# Gujarat Police PSI — co-priority

**Status: co-priority with CDS, not a background workstream.** This is your call and it is a
defensible one — see "Why this ranking is reasonable" below.

---

## Exam structure (current GPRB cycle)

There is **no separate prelims** in the current cycle. The route is:

**PST (physical standards) → PET (physical efficiency) → Main Written → Medical → Documents**

| Paper | Format | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 — General Studies | MCQ | 200 |
| Paper 2 — Gujarati & English | **Descriptive**, evaluated by a committee | 100 |
| **Total** | | **300** |

**PET (male): 5000 m in 25 minutes.** Qualifying, pass/fail.

⚠️ Verify all of this against the actual GPRB notification when it drops — structure has
changed between cycles, and it will change again.

---

## Three rules that decide this exam

### 1. There is an Option E, and forgetting it costs you marks

Negative marking is 0.25 per wrong answer — **and 0.25 for any question left completely blank.**
Every question carries a fifth option, **E — "Not Attempted"**. Selecting E costs nothing.
Marking nothing at all costs 0.25.

This is unlike CDS and unlike SSC, where skipping is free. Anyone drilled on those exams walks
in with the habit of leaving blanks and **bleeds marks for questions they never even read.**

> **On every single question: answer it, or mark E. Never leave a row empty.**

Practise this on OMR sheets until it is automatic. It is worth more than any chapter.

### 2. 40% minimum in each part of Paper 1, independently

You cannot carry a strong section over a weak one. A brilliant GK score does not rescue a failed
Constitution section. **Every part has to clear on its own** — which means no part can be punted,
and the weakest one sets your fate.

### 3. Paper 2 is handwritten and human-marked

A descriptive Gujarati and English paper, read by a committee. No amount of MCQ practice
prepares for it. Handwriting, structure, and formal register all count, and they are trained
only by actually writing.

## What PSI needs that CDS and CGL do not

This is the honest extra cost of making PSI co-priority — roughly **5–6 hours a week**:

| Workstream | Why it is PSI-only |
|---|---|
| **Gujarat GK** | History, geography, culture, economy, institutions of Gujarat |
| **Formal Gujarati** | Reading register *and* descriptive writing (see `gujarati.md`) |
| **Constitution & legal matters** | IPC/CrPC/BNS basics, police powers, evidence |
| **Psychology & Sociology** | Unusual on a police paper. Genuinely tested. Do not skip it |
| **PET readiness, continuously** | Notification → PET is a short gap. There is no time to get fit later |

Everything else — general studies, reasoning, science, current affairs, English — is shared
with the CDS and CGL core you are already building.

## Why this ranking is reasonable

Making PSI co-equal with CDS is not sentiment. The numbers support it:

- **858 PSI posts** in a single Gujarat cycle, against **451 CDS vacancies nationally**
- **No SSB.** CDS's real filter is a five-day interview with single-digit conversion. PSI's
  filter is a written paper and a run — both things that respond directly to work you control
- It is a **field job in your own state**, which is what you actually said you wanted

The one argument that still favours doing CDS first is the age window, and it is unchanged:
CDS closes for you around 2029; PSI stays open to 35. **Co-priority, with CDS holding the
deep block, is the sequencing that honours both.**

## The standing readiness posture

PSI has no date. That does not make it opportunistic — it makes it a **readiness problem**,
which is harder, because there is no deadline to react to.

When GPRB notifies, it is a 60–90 day sprint from a standing start. Candidates who begin
Gujarat GK and Gujarati on notification day have already lost to the ones who did ten minutes
a day for a year.

**Standing posture, maintained continuously:**

- [ ] 5 km comfortably under 23 minutes, year-round — never let this lapse
- [ ] Gujarati vocabulary log running daily
- [ ] One formal Gujarati paragraph written per week (from December)
- [ ] Gujarat GK covered once, then on monthly revision
- [ ] Constitution and legal basics covered once
- [ ] `gprb.gujarat.gov.in` and `ojas.gujarat.gov.in` checked on the 1st of every month
- [ ] Documents ready: degree, domicile, caste certificate if applicable, ID

The point of the posture is that a notification finds this **already done**, and the 60–90 days
go entirely into revision and mocks instead of starting from zero.
