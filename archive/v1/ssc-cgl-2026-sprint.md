# SSC CGL 2026 — the live one

## ⚠️ Step 1, today: confirm the application actually went through

You said "99%". That is precisely the number that turns into a wasted cycle, because the most
common failure is **a form that was filled but never paid for.** SSC does not count an unpaid
application as submitted, and candidates discover this on admit-card day.

1. Go to `ssc.gov.in` → Login
2. Registration ID + password
3. Open **My Applications** / application status for CGLE 2026
4. Confirm it reads **submitted** AND **fee paid** (or fee-exempt)
5. Screenshot it. Save the registration number into `reference/`

**If it did not go through:** nothing is lost from the main plan. CGL 2027 notification is
~May 2027, and by then your core will be far stronger than it is now. Move straight to the
CDS track and treat this as a free lesson about deadlines.

**If it did go through:** read on. You have a real exam in weeks.

## What Tier 1 actually is

| Section | Q | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| General Intelligence & Reasoning | 25 | 50 |
| General Awareness | 25 | 50 |
| Quantitative Aptitude | 25 | 50 |
| English Comprehension | 25 | 50 |
| **Total** | **100** | **200** |

**60 minutes. Negative marking 0.50 per wrong answer.**

Sixty minutes for a hundred questions is **36 seconds per question.** That single number should
reshape everything about how you prepare for it. This is not an exam of knowledge, it is an exam
of speed and triage. People who know more but decide slower score less.

---

## The sprint — 2 to 6 weeks from near-zero

You cannot learn this syllabus in the time available. **You can learn the exam**, and that is
worth far more marks than half-learning the content.

**Priority order, highest yield first:**

1. **Reasoning (25 Q)** — the best return per hour for a beginner. It is pattern recognition, not
   content: analogy, series, coding-decoding, blood relations, direction, syllogism. A few dozen
   hours makes most of this section reliable. Spend the most time here.
2. **Quantitative (25 Q)** — you are an engineer, the maths is not the obstacle; **speed is.**
   Drill arithmetic shortcuts, percentage-fraction conversions, and squares/cubes to 30. Do not
   study new concepts, drill fast execution of familiar ones.
3. **English (25 Q)** — grammar rules and vocabulary give quick, cheap marks. Error spotting,
   fill-in-the-blanks, synonyms/antonyms, idioms.
4. **General Awareness (25 Q)** — **deliberately last.** Static GK is enormous and cannot be
   compressed. Take current affairs of the last 6 months plus basic science, and accept losing
   marks here. Chasing GA in a sprint is the classic way to lose the other three sections.

**Non-negotiable:** at least **five full mocks under real 60-minute timing** before exam day.
Timing pressure is the actual skill being tested, and it cannot be learned from books.

## Triage strategy in the hall

- Two passes. First pass: answer only what you see immediately. Skip anything that needs
  thinking. Second pass: return to the skipped ones with the clock in view.
- Negative marking is 0.50 — steeper than CDS. **Guess only when two options are eliminated.**
- Never let one question eat 90 seconds. That question costs you three easier ones.

## Whatever the score

**Sit it regardless of how prepared you feel.** One day, and it buys:

- A real, scored diagnostic across four sections, taken under genuine pressure
- Exam-hall experience — the biometrics, the nerves, the clock, the shift timing
- Zero cost to the CDS timeline

A bad first CGL score is not a setback. Walking into CDS I 2027 having never sat a competitive
exam under real conditions **is.**
