# Gujarati — the exam-register problem

## Your actual gap

From the GPSC ICT Officer paper you sat: you **read the Gujarati GS paper without difficulty**
but did not know the meaning of many words.

That is a precise, narrow, and very fixable diagnosis. You do not have a literacy problem.
You have a **vocabulary-register problem** — specifically પારિભાષિક શબ્દો, the formal,
administrative, technical and Sanskritised Gujarati that appears in government papers and
never in conversation.

This matters because it is invisible until it costs you an exam. Nobody fails to notice they
can't read. Everybody fails to notice they don't know the formal word for "jurisdiction"
until it is in the question stem.

## The fix — 10 minutes a day, starting today

The cheapest intervention in this entire plan. It costs 10 minutes and removes a gap that
can cost a whole paper.

1. **Read one Gujarati editorial daily.** Divya Bhaskar, Gujarat Samachar or Sandesh —
   the **editorial and national pages**, not sports or entertainment. Editorial Gujarati
   is written in exactly the register the exams use.
2. **Keep a vocabulary log.** `log/gujarati-vocab.md`. Every unfamiliar word:
   word · meaning · the sentence you found it in. Context is what makes it stick.
3. **Revise the log weekly.** Sunday, 10 minutes. Words you have seen three times are yours.
4. **Learn the domain sets deliberately** — these recur constantly in police and
   administrative papers:
   - Government & administration — ministries, departments, offices, designations
   - Legal & police — offence, investigation, jurisdiction, custody, warrant, evidence
   - Constitutional — rights, duties, articles, amendments, federal structure
   - Economy & budget terms
   - Geography & environment terms

## Writing — Phase 2 onward

PSI Mains has a **descriptive Gujarati paper**. Reading fluency does not produce writing
fluency; that has to be trained separately.

From December: **one paragraph of formal Gujarati per week.** Letter, précis, or short essay.
Do not start this now — it is a PSI-specific need and PSI has no date yet. The vocabulary
drip is what has to start today, because vocabulary takes months to build and writing takes weeks.

## Why this runs all year even though PSI has no date

When a GPRB notification drops, it is a 60–90 day sprint from a standing start. Candidates
who begin Gujarati vocabulary on notification day have already lost. Ten minutes a day for a
year means that when the notification comes, this workstream is simply **already done.**
